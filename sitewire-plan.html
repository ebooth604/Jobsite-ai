Initial business plan · Vancouver, BC · working title

SitewireJobsite AI that counts what the crew installed — and proves what it wasn't paid for.

Production intelligence for mid-market specialty subcontractors. Every funded jobsite-AI company sells visibility into the sub's work, to the general contractor. Nobody sells it to the sub — the party that carries the labour risk, eats the rework, and loses the change-order argument for lack of evidence. And in British Columbia, that argument just acquired a statutory deadline.

01Thesis

Subs measure hours precisely and installed quantity not at all

A specialty sub bids a job as quantity × labour productivity: N linear feet of conduit at X hours per hundred. Everything after the bid is a race between what was assumed and what actually happens. The sub tracks one side of that ratio to the penny — payroll — and the other side not at all.

So a job that's going to lose 400 hours announces itself in week three and is discovered in week eight, when the recoverable window has already closed. And when the money argument starts, the GC's version of site conditions is better-evidenced than the sub's.

Sitewire turns the photos crews already take into installed-quantity tracking, joins it to their labour hours and bid rates, and assembles the evidence package that gets the out-of-scope work paid for.
02Why here, why now

BC just rewrote the rules on getting paid

The Construction Prompt Payment Act (Bill 20, 2025) imposes 28-day payment on proper invoices, cuts the Builders Lien Act holdback release period from 55 days to 46, and introduces statutory adjudication — fast dispute resolution on tight clocks. Regulations went to industry consultation in June 2026.

A regime that resolves payment disputes in weeks instead of years pays whoever walks in with dated, contemporaneous documentation. BC subcontractors are about to need exactly the artifact we produce, on a deadline — and most of them keep that evidence in a text thread.

This is a real home-market advantage, not a location detail: the same product is worth more in BC in 2027 than it is in Texas, because the law makes the evidence actionable on a schedule. It is also the sharpest single point of failure in the plan — see §09.

04Product

One loop, five steps

No new hardware. No 360° rig, no drone, no BIM model — deliberate constraints, because our buyer has none of those things and our competitors all require at least one.

01CaptureCrews shoot photos and 30-second walk videos on the phone in their pocket — or we ingest the photo stream already flowing into Procore or Autodesk Build.
02ExtractVision models estimate installed quantities against the bid's scope items, locate them by area, and flag conditions worth remembering: blocked access, stacked trades, unfinished predecessor work, damage.
03ReconcileInstalled quantity joins that day's labour hours and the bid's budgeted units-per-hour. Output: a productivity factor per scope item, per crew, per day.
04Alert"Level 2 branch conduit has run at 0.71 of bid productivity for six working days. At this rate the scope finishes 320 hours over. Top correlated condition: ceiling grid installed ahead of rough-in in 4 of 6 areas."
05DocumentWhen a condition is billable, the system assembles a dated, geolocated, photo-backed package in the shape the change order — or a Bill 20 adjudication submission — actually needs.

Step 5 is what gets us renewed. Steps 3–4 are what make us hard to replace. In BC, step 5 has a statutory clock, which is why our build order puts it earlier than a US-first plan would.

05Privacy

Worker privacy is a product decision, not a legal afterthought

Jobsite photos contain workers' faces, and in BC that makes them personal information under PIPA. BC is also a heavily unionized construction market. A product that reads to the Building Trades as worker surveillance is dead on arrival regardless of its legal footing — and deservedly so.

Committed in the product and in the contract
  • We measure installed work, not workers. No individual productivity metric exists in the data model — not hidden, not gated, absent. The unit of analysis is the scope item and the crew, never the person.
  • Faces blurred at ingest, before storage, original discarded.
  • Canadian data residency by default, so contractors on public work — health authorities, school districts, BC Hydro, TransLink — can answer their FIPPA questions without calling us.
  • A plain-language worker notice the customer can post on site.

Treat this as a moat rather than a cost: a competitor that started with GC-facing surveillance features cannot credibly adopt this stance later.

06Customer

The mid-market specialty sub, Lower Mainland first

Firm

Self-performing specialty subcontractor, $10–150M revenue, 8–40 concurrent projects, ICI and high-rise residential.

Geography

Metro Vancouver and the Fraser Valley — the Lower Mainland carries roughly 60% of BC's construction employment.

Economic buyer

VP of Operations or President. At this size that person still knows every job by name and personally feels a losing one.

Champion

Chief Estimator or Director of PM — owns the bid productivity rates we measure against, and has wanted this feedback loop their entire career.

Why they buy — in the order that actually closes deals

  1. "You found $180K of billable out-of-scope work on one job that we would have eaten."
  2. "You told us job 24-118 was going sideways in week three."
  3. "When the adjudication notice landed, our package took an afternoon instead of three weeks." A BC reason that did not exist before 2026.
  4. "Our foremen stopped writing daily reports and the reports got better." Adoption lubricant, not a purchase reason.

07Competition

Why we're not roadkill

The reality-capture category is well capitalized and largely won at the GC and owner tier — Buildots raised a $45M Series D, Trunk Tools $30.5M, Fyld $41M. We are not attacking it. We're arguing the sub is a separate market: separate buyer, separate data (labour hours and bid units, which the GC never sees), separate value event.

Defensibility, ranked by how much we actually believe it

  1. Data. Installed quantity ↔ labour hours ↔ bid rate, by trade, across firms. Nobody else has both sides of that ratio, because nobody else sells to the party that owns both.
  2. Workflow lock. Once change orders are documented in our format and estimators tune bid rates against our history, we're inside the annual planning cycle.
  3. Trust in the trade. Metro Vancouver's subcontracting community is small and referral-driven. That compounds fast here — and works against us later, when we're unknown in Seattle.
  4. The privacy stance. Hard for a GC-facing incumbent to adopt without contradicting its own product.
  5. Model quality. Real, but a depreciating asset. We should not tell ourselves this is the moat.

Where we lose: if Procore ships adequate quantity extraction as a bundled feature before we own the ratio dataset.

08Vancouver

The base: what it buys us, what it costs us

Cash-efficient R&D

As a CCPC: 35% federal refundable SR&ED credit — recently enhanced to a $6M expenditure limit — plus BC's 10% refundable credit, on top of IRAP. A large fraction of our biggest cost line comes back as cash, not a deduction. The strongest financial argument for building here.

Regulatory timing

Bill 20's adjudication regime lands as we launch. No US state offers this tailwind in 2027.

Talent and currency

Deep CV bench from UBC/SFU and the local VFX and games industry, well below Bay Area cost. CAD cost base against USD revenue the moment we cross into Washington.

A walkable beachhead

A founder can stand in four jobsite trailers in one day. The design-partner phase depends on exactly that.

Cost: the home market is small

BC has ~28,225 construction companies, but our ICP band is a few hundred firms province-wide. Vancouver is a beachhead, never the business.

Cost: thinner local capital

BC's pre-seed market is smaller than Seattle's. Expect local angels with construction money plus US funds — so the US expansion story belongs in the first raise, not after it.

10Pricing

Per active project, not per seat

Seat pricing punishes the customer for putting more crew on capture — exactly the behaviour our data quality depends on. Project pricing scales with delivered value and matches how a sub already thinks about cost coding.

US list at near-numeric parity is a deliberate ~25–30% real price increase on cross-border deals — standard in this market, and it helps fund US go-to-market.

Explicitly not doing

Contingency pricing on recovered change orders and adjudication awards. It's the pricing the value story begs for, and it turns us into a claims-consulting firm with software attached: unsellable multiple, adversarial relationship with the GC ecosystem, and possibly near the line on providing legal services in BC. Revisit only with counsel.

11Go to market

Founder-led, trade-vertical, Lower Mainland dense

M0Design partners — months 0–6Six Metro Vancouver subs, two trades. Free-to-cheap in exchange for capture access, labour data, and brutal weekly feedback. Success = 70%+ crew-day capture and one documented dollar recovery per partner.
M6Paid pilots — months 6–12Convert design partners; add 10–12 paid pilots in the Lower Mainland. Every pilot ends in a written accuracy-and-value review — the sales asset for the next ten.
M12Cascadia — months 12–30Victoria and the Interior are marginal; the real second market is Seattle, then Calgary. Third trade, first two AEs hired from the trade rather than from SaaS.

Channels, in the order we'd work them

  • VRCA — the single highest-leverage room in the beachhead.
  • ECABC, MCABC, BCCA, ICBA — by discipline and by shop.
  • The Bill 20 moment. Every association, law firm, and surety in the province is running prompt-payment education through the regulation rollout. A genuinely good explainer on what documentation an adjudication actually requires is the cheapest qualified-lead source we will ever have. Do it as education, not as a pitch.
  • Construction lawyers and claims consultants — they see the evidence gap first, and they're the ones who tell a sub "you'd have won this if you'd photographed it."
  • Surety brokers and construction accountants, structurally interested in their clients not losing money on jobs.

Non-dilutive offset. Engineering dominates the burn and most of it is SR&ED-eligible, so a substantial share of qualifying salary comes back as cash on a 12–18 month lag. Model it conservatively and treat the refund as runway extension, never as revenue — and get the eligibility position reviewed before the raise, because claiming productization work as research is the classic way a startup's claim gets reduced.

Early gross margin is deliberately poor: inference cost per project-day plus human QA on quantity extraction. The margin curve is the technical milestone. If QA cost per project doesn't halve between M12 and M24, the model isn't learning and we're a services firm.

Metrics that decide the seed

Net revenue retention above 120% via project expansion · crew-day capture above 70% · quantity accuracy within ±10% on trained scope items · corrections-per-project-week trending down · documented dollar recovery per account.

15Open

Questions we haven't answered

  1. Trade choice. Electrical + concrete forming is a guess tuned to this market. Electrical has the best quantity structure and worst rough-in visibility; forming is most visually tractable but exposed to the residential curve. Mechanical piping may beat both in BC's institutional pipeline.
  2. How hard do we lean on Bill 20? It's the sharpest "why now" we have and the sharpest single point of failure. Leaning on it wins meetings in 2027 and dates the company if adjudication uptake disappoints.
  3. Do we need the bid takeoff? The ratio needs budgeted units. If subs won't share takeoffs early, we need a cold-start path — crew-relative trending instead of bid-relative.
  4. When do we cross the border? Month 24 is a placeholder. Earlier de-risks the sizing story; later keeps focus. Vancouver pilot conversion should decide it.
  5. CCPC status vs. US investors. The SR&ED advantage is real money and it depends on control staying Canadian. Take a US lead too early and we trade a durable cost advantage for a cheque. Get the structure advice before the raise.
  6. Where does safety fit? Easiest to detect, hardest to charge a sub for, and here it collides directly with our privacy stance. Probably never the wedge.
