Initial business plan · working title

SitewireJobsite AI that counts what the crew installed — and proves what it wasn't paid for.

Production intelligence for mid-market specialty subcontractors. Every funded jobsite-AI company sells visibility into the sub's work, to the general contractor. Nobody sells it to the sub — the party that carries the labor risk, eats the rework, and loses the change-order argument for lack of evidence.

01Thesis

Subs measure hours precisely and installed quantity not at all

A specialty sub bids a job as quantity × labor productivity: N linear feet of conduit at X hours per hundred. Everything after the bid is a race between what was assumed and what actually happens. The sub tracks one side of that ratio to the penny — payroll — and the other side not at all.

So a job that's going to lose 400 hours announces itself in week three and is discovered in week eight, when the recoverable window has already closed. And when the money argument starts, the GC's version of site conditions is better-evidenced than the sub's.

Sitewire turns the photos crews already take into installed-quantity tracking, joins it to their labor hours and bid rates, and assembles the evidence package that gets the out-of-scope work paid for.

03Product

One loop, five steps

No new hardware. No 360° rig, no drone, no BIM model — deliberate constraints, because our buyer has none of those things and our competitors all require at least one.

01CaptureCrews shoot photos and 30-second walk videos on the phone in their pocket — or we ingest the photo stream already flowing into Procore or Autodesk Build.
02ExtractVision models estimate installed quantities against the bid's scope items, locate them by area, and flag conditions worth remembering: blocked access, stacked trades, unfinished predecessor work, damage.
03ReconcileInstalled quantity joins that day's labor hours and the bid's budgeted units-per-hour. Output: a productivity factor per scope item, per crew, per day.
04Alert"Level 2 branch conduit has run at 0.71 of bid productivity for six working days. At this rate the scope finishes 320 hours over. Top correlated condition: ceiling grid installed ahead of rough-in in 4 of 6 areas."
05DocumentWhen a condition is billable, the system assembles a dated, geolocated, photo-backed evidence package in the shape the change order or delay claim needs.

Step 5 is what gets us renewed. Steps 3–4 are what make us hard to replace.

Two trades, not ten. Quantity models are trade-specific, and credibility with a $40M electrical contractor requires being right, not broad.

Trust commitments in v1
  • Every number traces back to the photos that produced it, in one click.
  • The model reports a confidence band and abstains rather than guessing — an abstention is a 30-second nudge to the foreman, never a silent bad number.
  • Any quantity is correctable in one tap, and corrections are the training signal. Corrections-per-week is our core quality metric and must fall month over month per customer.
04Customer

The mid-market specialty sub

Firm

US specialty trade subcontractor, $10–150M revenue, 8–40 concurrent projects, self-performing labor, commercial / institutional / industrial.

Economic buyer

VP of Operations or President. At this size that person still knows every job by name and personally feels a losing one.

Champion

Chief Estimator or Director of PM — owns the bid productivity rates we measure against, and has wanted this feedback loop their entire career.

Users

Foreman, 30 seconds a day of capture. PM, five minutes a day of reading.

Disqualified: brokers who don't self-perform; residential production builders; firms under ~$8M with no PM layer; firms whose GC mandates a competing platform and whose contracts strip change-order rights.

Why they buy — in the order that actually closes deals

  1. "You found $180K of billable out-of-scope work on one job that we would have eaten." Pays for itself, undeniably.
  2. "You told us job 24-118 was going sideways in week three." The ops leader's recurring nightmare.
  3. "Our foremen stopped writing daily reports and the reports got better." Adoption lubricant, not a purchase reason.

05Competition

Why we're not roadkill

The reality-capture category is well capitalized and largely won at the GC and owner tier — Buildots raised a $45M Series D, Trunk Tools $30.5M, Fyld $41M. We are not attacking it. We're arguing the sub is a separate market: separate buyer, separate data (labor hours and bid units, which the GC never sees), separate value event.

Defensibility, ranked by how much we actually believe it

  1. Data. Installed quantity ↔ labor hours ↔ bid rate, by trade, across firms. Nobody else has both sides of that ratio, because nobody else sells to the party that owns both. Compounds, genuinely hard to copy.
  2. Workflow lock. Once change orders are documented in our format and estimators tune bid rates against our history, we're inside the annual planning cycle.
  3. Trust in the trade. Subcontracting is a referral market. Ten happy electrical contractors in one metro beats a hundred scattered logos.
  4. Model quality. Real, but a depreciating asset. We should not tell ourselves this is the moat.

Where we lose: if Procore ships adequate quantity extraction as a bundled feature before we own the ratio dataset. Mitigation is speed and depth in two trades — and being an obvious acquisition rather than an obstacle.

06Pricing

Per active project, not per seat

Seat pricing punishes the customer for putting more crew on capture — exactly the behavior our data quality depends on. Project pricing scales with delivered value and matches how a sub already thinks about cost coding.

Explicitly not doing

Contingency pricing on recovered change orders. It's the pricing the value story begs for, and it makes us a claims-consulting firm with software attached — unsellable multiple, adversarial relationship with the GC ecosystem, compliance mess in some states. Revisit only with counsel.

07Go to market

Founder-led, trade-vertical, metro-dense

Not PLG. Not inside sales at this stage. The motion compounds through referral inside one trade in one metro before it goes anywhere else.

M0Design partners — months 0–6Six subs, one metro, two trades. Free-to-cheap in exchange for capture access, labor data, and brutal weekly feedback. Success = 70%+ crew-day capture and one documented dollar recovery per partner.
M6Paid pilots — months 6–12Convert design partners; add 10–12 paid pilots in the same metro and trade. Every pilot ends in a written accuracy-and-value review — that document is the sales asset for the next ten.
M12Metro expansion — months 12–24Second and third metro, third trade, first two AEs hired from the trade rather than from SaaS. Association speaking, trade-press cases, and an annual Subcontractor Productivity Benchmark built from our own dataset.

Channel bets worth testing, not building on: timekeeping vendors as referral partners; regional NECA / ABC chapters; construction accounting firms and surety brokers, who are structurally interested in their clients not losing money on jobs.

11Open

Questions we haven't answered

  1. Trade choice. Electrical + concrete is a guess. Electrical has the best quantity structure and the worst rough-in visibility; concrete is the most visually tractable but the shortest scope duration. Mechanical piping may beat both. Decide with discovery data, not preference.
  2. Do we need the bid takeoff? The ratio needs budgeted units. If subs won't share takeoffs early, we need a cold-start path — crew-relative trending instead of bid-relative.
  3. Where does safety fit? Easiest thing to detect, hardest thing to charge a sub for. Probably a retention feature, never the wedge.
  4. Buy or build the capture app? A thin layer on an existing capture SDK may beat building our own for twelve months.
  5. Is the GC eventually the better buyer anyway? Hold it open and let pilot economics answer, rather than defending the sub thesis past the point of evidence.
